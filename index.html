The bol keyboard

Thirteen strokes mapped across the home row. Right-hand dayan strokes ring on tuned overtones, the left-hand bayan carries the bass glide, and crisp finger taps articulate fast phrases. Enter triggers the four-stroke tirakita roll.

How each stroke is synthesized

Ringing dayan strokes sum five damped sine modes whose frequency ratios sit near a harmonic series, modeling how the black syahi paste loads the goat-skin head toward a clear pitch. The openness parameter controls upper-mode survival, separating a bright rim na from a full open tin or tun.

The left-hand bayan adds a downward pitch envelope modeling wrist pressure across the larger membrane. Dry strokes pass filtered noise bursts through two short resonators, and a single tirakita trigger schedules four sub-hits 55 milliseconds apart on the audio thread.

        About this practice room

        TaalTrainer is a self-contained browser practice instrument built for keeping cyclic time and hearing how spoken bol syllables map onto right-hand and left-hand strokes. The four loop patterns are practice adaptations tailored to the thirteen synthesized voices on this page.

        Every sound is synthesized locally at the moment you strike a key, from damped sine modes and shaped noise inside Web Audio worklets. Zero audio samples are fetched, zero network calls leave the browser, and all 162 automated verification tests run offline.
