The bol keyboard

Thirteen strokes on the home row. Right hand strokes ring, the left hand speaks bass, and the dry finger taps carry the fast work. Enter fires the whole tirakita roll. Hold nothing, press everything.

How each stroke is built

Ringing strokes sum five damped sine modes whose ratios sit near a harmonic series, the way the syahi load pulls a real drum head toward its tuning note. Openness decides how much of the upper modes survive, which is the whole difference between an open tin and a closed na. Higher modes decay faster than the fundamental.

The bass hand adds a pitch glide, a short slide downward as the heel of the hand presses the bayan. Dry strokes are noise bursts through two very short damped modes. A tirakita is four such hits, 55 milliseconds apart, fired from one key press.

        About this trainer

        TaalTrainer is a practice tool, not a method and not a lineage. It teaches through its own twelve lessons and the thekas built into the taal player. The thekas are practice lines adapted to the app's thirteen strokes, meant for keeping time, not for repertoire. It is built for daily practice, one honest stroke at a time, and it makes no claims beyond what is written on this page.

        Every sound is synthesized locally at the moment you play, from sine modes and shaped noise. No audio files are loaded, no network request leaves the page, and nothing you play is stored anywhere.
